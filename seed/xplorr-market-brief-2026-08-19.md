# Xplorr market brief
Date: 19 August 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Sources 2024–2026 unless noted. No invented figures. Company-claimed numbers are labelled. Vendor TAM reports are labelled as such.

Lead product (public raise story): rugged sampling UAV that retrieves rock/soil up to 10 kg. National titles/geology map is secondary.

## 1. What Xplorr sells today

Homepage (https://www.xplorr.ai/) positions the product as a remote prospecting tool, not a camera drone.

Company claims on that page (not independently verified):
- One operator, no helicopters.
- Precision claw collects rock or soil up to 10 kg.
- Target → retrieve → return in minutes; sample bagged/tagged for assay.
- Helicopter alternative: “$2,000+ per hour”.
- Traditional walk-in: 2–3 spots/day vs Xplorr 10+ spots/day; 15-minute flight from a vehicle.
- Pilbara field result: 14 high-value targets sampled in 3 days vs originally planned 21 days; “$75k+ saved vs helicopter support”. Attributed to “Lawrence Smith, Senior Project Geologist”.
- Complimentary “Exploration ROI Report” sales motion.

Solutions page (https://www.xplorr.ai/solutions/) also describes:
- Autonomous drones “repurposed from Ukrainian kamikaze drones and retrofitted with a sample retrieval apparatus”.
- Samples + imagery feed AI “Exploration Potential” and “Drilling Target” models that use government/open data (page names GeoView).
- Outputs: heatmaps, ranked drill targets, 3D visualisations.

Tracxn (2026 profile): Sydney, founded 2024, listed as unfunded. https://tracxn.com/d/companies/xplorrai/__q748xzqqOP7wOFyQudsKx1jNRzUyghBBL8bSiWr89Ec

## 4. How hardware + service companies actually get paid

Patterns that have raised or listed:

1. **Payload + software subscription** (Emesent Hovermap + Aura/Cortex). Capex to the mine or survey contractor; recurring processing/autonomy.
2. **Drone-in-a-box + remote operations contract** (RocketDNA xBot). Monthly/multi-year; CASA approval is the gating item. FY25 revenue still only A$7.7m after a decade — scale is slow.
3. **Survey campaign + sensors** (Fleet Space ExoSphere). Hardware on the ground, satellite backhaul, AI targeting. Series D at A$800m+ is the outlier; they sell **new subsurface data**, not a UI on state portals.
4. **Sensors in the workflow the customer already pays for** (IMDEX downhole tools → Datarock AI logs). Digital is a bolt-on to metres drilled.
5. **Specialist service, custom payload** (RemSense water sampling for BHP). Project fees; hard to productise.

Xplorr’s stated motion is closer to (5) plus a productised airframe: complimentary ROI report → field campaign replacing heli/walk-in. Recurring would require either (a) unit sales/leases to explorers or (b) a sampling-as-a-service fleet.

CASA: RocketDNA’s Calidus contract was explicitly conditional on attaching the site to existing BVLOS/area approvals (IM-Mining 28 Mar 2024). Any AU sampling UAV that leaves VLOS or carries a heavy external load inherits the same regulator, not just the hardware problem.

## 7. Investor objections

### 7.1 Hardware: “this is a DJI with a claw”

What the comps show the moat actually is when hardware wins:

| Moat | Who used it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **GPS-denied autonomy / SLAM IP** | Emesent, Exyn, Flyability | A$25m + A$32m (Emesent); Nasdaq listing (Exyn); CHF 22m Series C (Flyability) |
| **New geophysical data, not public data** | Fleet Space sensors + satellites | A$150m Series D @ A$800m+ |
| **Installed in the existing paid workflow** | IMDEX sensors → Datarock AI | Staged ~A$5.5m then ~A$31m buyout |
| **Regulator + remote-ops licence** | RocketDNA CASA BVLOS / ROC | Listed, A$4m raise, still A$7.7m revenue |
| **Safety / confined-space hardware** | Flyability cage; RemSense tether release | Sold as removing humans from pits/voids |
| **Manufacturing + fleet** | Emesent Wacol; RocketDNA xBot inventory | NRFC debt specifically for manufacturing |

Xplorr’s public differentiators: **10 kg claw**, **heli displacement**, **one operator**, **Pilbara gossan case**. Risks a buyer will test: CASA external-load / BVLOS, sample integrity (rotor wash, contamination — Honeybee paper flags rotor-wash protection), payload vs endurance, wind in Pilbara, assay chain-of-custody, and whether a DJI-class airframe + gripper is protectable. Solutions-page “Ukrainian kamikaze” origin is a supply/export-control talking point, not a moat.

### 7.2 Map: “this is a UI on public government data”

See Appendix B. Short version: **NextMaps is the live AU example of charging for a stitch of free WA data** (A$50–200/user/mo) and they say so on the homepage: “MTO and TENGRAPH are free, but you do the reading.” **Minerva Intelligence** sold its geology-AI division to Seequent for **C$1m** (Dec 2022) and left the category — a failed standalone public-data/AI geology story. Fleet Space and IMDEX/Datarock raised or sold on **private/new measurements + models**, not portal UX.

## APPENDIX B — Companies that won or failed on public geoscience data

**Won (narrowly, on workflow):**
- **NextMaps** — charges for reading the free WA register + WAMEX + constraints, with alerts and AI desk studies. Explicitly WA-only. Price is public (above). Closest product analog to a “stitched government map.”
- **Mineflow** — free core tools; enterprise adds AU/Ivory Coast tenement feeds and ASX/TSX live feed.
- **Geoscience Australia GeoInsight / RAP** — government doing the national stitch itself, funded **A$3.4bn / 35 years**. Competes with any commercial “national map” on price (free) and mandate.

**Failed / exited as a standalone geology-AI story:**
- **Minerva Intelligence** (TSXV:MVAI) sold DRIVER geology division to Seequent/Bentley for **C$1 million** (agreement 5 Dec 2022; close 9 Dec 2022). Company pivoted to climate risk, later renamed Aisix. https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/minerva-to-focus-on-climate-risk-platform-climate85-and-announces-sale-of-geology-division-864801089.html

**Won by not being a public-data UI:**
- Fleet Space — proprietary seismic + satellites.
- IMDEX/Datarock — core imagery the miner already paid to drill.
- Seequent/Micromine — models that become the JORC resource.

Usual real moat (from the above, not theory): **workflow lock-in, private or newly acquired data, models trained on that data, hardware that creates the data, or compliance/licence (CASA, JORC, tenement statutory systems).** A national viewer of state portals is the NextMaps job, priced like a SaaS seat, not a robotics raise.

## 8. Working conclusion for a raise (facts only)

- Public story is a **10 kg sampling UAV** replacing heli/walk-in first-pass sampling. Category is real (heli is expensive; RemSense proves AU majors will buy **sampling** UAVs) but **almost empty of funded product peers**.
- Capital has gone to mining drones that **map, inspect, or image the subsurface** (Emesent A$57m disclosed across 2022+2026, Fleet Space A$150m D, RocketDNA A$4m + listing, Flyability CHF 22m, Exyn IPO). None of those rounds were for a rock-claw.
- AU demand backdrop is supportive: ~750 listed explorers, A$3.9bn exploration spend in 2025, record junior cash (A$12.04bn, Dec 2025), greenfield still >A$1bn/year (2024).
- Comparable listed AU mining-drone **service** business (RocketDNA) is A$7.7m revenue and still loss-making. That is the honest scale reference for “drones for miners” as a service.
- Map/tenement stitch is a **A$50–200/user/mo** business (NextMaps) sitting on free government data; Minerva’s geology AI exited at C$1m. Do not price the map like Leapfrog (Bentley ~US$1.05bn) or Micromine (Weir A$1.31bn).
